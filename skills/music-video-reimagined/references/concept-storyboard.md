# Phase 3: the concept and the storyboard

## Find the big idea
A great reimagining is not the original video with Clawds pasted in. It's the song's meaning plus the video's visual
language, translated into this medium: a painted paper world of watercolour and ink, and blocky clay characters.

1. **The meaning** (your paraphrase from the source study): what does the song *feel* like?
2. **The medium**: the world is painted on paper, lit by glows, drawn in ink that boils. What can paper, ink, paint,
   shadows, lamps or the page itself *do*?
3. **The device**: find one literal metaphor that joins the two, something that can happen early in a small way and
   escalate to the climax. On Papercut the song is about something under your skin, the world is paper, so a
   *papercut* is a slit in the sheet itself, and behind it is the Ink Side where the band's dark doubles live.

Brainstorm several devices before choosing. Some starting prompts:
- Songs about memory → paint that fades, washes out or runs in the rain.
- Songs about love or longing → two drawings on different pages, a fold that brings them together.
- Songs about self-image → reflections, shadows or doubles that disagree with their owner.
- Songs about freedom or escape → a character stepping off the edge of the paper, or the frame itself.
- Songs about chaos, parties or dance → ink spills that become dancers, or a room that won't stay still.
- Songs about time → the page ageing, a spinning record or clock, the same room across time.
- Songs about fame or pressure → a stage, spotlights and a crowd of identical Clawds.

The device must serve the song's emotional arc, not just look clever.

**Other devices that worked:**
- **Contagion.** A dance or feeling spreads from character to character to the whole world. Every shot's
  surprise is *who* catches it next ("Gangnam Style": a kid → grannies → a traffic light → the Moon).
- **A true-story easter egg.** Look up what the song did in the real world. "Gangnam Style" was the first video to
  overflow a 32-bit view counter. That became a painted odometer tracking the contagion, overflowing to −2,147,483,648
  at the bridge and rebooting as 64-bit on the final drop. One shared function gives the count at any time, so every
  chapter shows the same continuous number.
- **The skeptic running gag.** One deadpan character appears in every scene, conspicuously NOT joining in
  (a horse, unimpressed by humans doing a "horse dance"). It pays off when it finally joins, better than everyone,
  and it's the perfect last laugh in a silent tail.

**Text and motion graphics.** The kit's default is no text. If the user explicitly allows text or motion graphics,
use them as *designed elements*: a counter, a banner, painted sound effects, floor numbers. Never use them as captions
of what's happening, and **never for lyrics** (that rule doesn't change).

## Keep the homage
List 5–8 things fans will recognise from the original (the set, a costume, a famous move, a gag, the ending) and
reinvent each one *through the device*. On Papercut the creepy portrait that changes became the ancestor portrait of
Great-Grand-Clawd, which ends with the Ink sitting in its ruff; the shadow copying the rapper became lamp shadows
that lag, peel off the wall and lead; the split set became a torn page.

## Reimagine the performers as Clawds
- Identity comes from **silhouette** (hair and headwear), **colour blocks** (clothes on the lower third of the body,
  patterned legs), **props** (instruments, mic), **proportions** (taller, stockier, a little wider) and **one acting
  trait** each (explosive, paranoid, calm, mischievous).
- Stay affectionate. These are caricatures in the kit's cute style, never realistic likenesses, and never mocking.
  For an artist who has died, keep the tribute respectful.
- Give each performer a dark double, rival or alter ego only if the concept needs one, and build it from the same
  design data so it stays on model.
- If the source video features a well-known copyrighted character (a famous cartoon, a mascot or a game character),
  don't draw it. Invent an original character in the Clawd world that plays the same role.

## Plan the whole film
- **Chapters follow the song's sections.** One chapter per section or pair of sections, 12–30 s each. A key ending
  gets its own chapter.
- **Escalation.** Each motif gets a row: how it appears in the intro, the verses, the choruses, the bridge, the finale
  and the ending. Every step must be a clear escalation.
- **Palette arc.** For example: warm lamplight → colder and greener as the tension grows → the dark world → a red
  climax → warm again, but *changed*.
- **The ending rhymes with the opening**: the same place, pose or motif, transformed. If the song ends hard, the
  silence after it is the perfect place for the twist.
- **Transitions at every seam**, and they belong to the story (cut on action, a push through a slit, a match cut on
  a shape, a whip, a flash, a tear). Specify each seam exactly in the storyboard, since two different builders own
  its two sides.
- **No text and no lyrics on screen**, ever. Act the meaning of a section; never write it.

understood before the next starts (see the kit's ANIMATION_GUIDE rule 4).

## Share it early
Send STORYBOARD.md to the user once it's written, and keep building unless they redirect you. It's cheap for them to
change direction now and expensive later.
